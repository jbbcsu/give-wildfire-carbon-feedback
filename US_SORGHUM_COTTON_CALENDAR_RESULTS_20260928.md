# U.S. sorghum and cotton fixed-calendar results

The pinned 2010 USDA NASS usual-date report supplies 17 all-cotton state
definitions and 14 sorghum-for-grain state definitions. Every state observed
in the acquired outcome panels is covered: Arkansas, Louisiana, Mississippi,
New Mexico, Oklahoma, and Texas for upland cotton; Colorado, Kansas, Nebraska,
New Mexico, Oklahoma, and Texas for sorghum.

Expanding the definitions over 1981--2018 and two frozen roles produces 2,356
calendar rows. All 8,771 exact paired crop--county--year outcome keys receive
both the primary most-active-midpoint window and the broad begin-to-end
sensitivity. Independent validation checks source-row counts, output hashes,
unique keys, chronology, duration, fixed month/day invariance, three manually
read source anchors, and exact outcome coverage.

This closes only the calendar-availability gate. The dates are state-level
usual dates published in 2010, not county-year realized phenology. NASS labels
the cotton table “All Cotton,” whereas the outcome series is upland cotton;
that scope difference must remain visible in interpretation. Both practices
receive the same calendar, so irrigation-specific timing is not identified.
Texas sorghum's statewide planting tail overlaps its harvest onset, consistent
with geographic aggregation; the construction uses internally ordered primary
and broad windows rather than pretending all eight bounds describe one farm.

No weather was joined and no response, causal irrigation effect, national
damage, or SCC was estimated.
