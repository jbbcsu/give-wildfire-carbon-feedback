# Sorghum/cotton predictive heterogeneity audit

The audit classifies all 168 prespecified state-fold comparisons as material
improvements, positive but below threshold, or worse. It combines the frozen
PDSI and direct-weather results without refitting any model.

New Mexico, Oklahoma, and Texas are the only states common to both crop panels.
Across five analogous non-irrigated comparisons and these three states (15
crop-paired records), only four show a material predictive improvement for both
crops, while seven have opposite signs of RMSE improvement across crops. For
example, direct-weather stage shares improve sorghum prediction but worsen
cotton prediction in New Mexico; the reverse holds in Texas. PDSI is somewhat
more concordant in New Mexico, but the overall transfer pattern remains mixed.

This is evidence of geographic and crop heterogeneity in predictive model
performance, not evidence that a state or crop is a climate-damage winner or
loser. Welfare winners and losers require validated response signs, future
exposure changes, production/value weights, and adaptation assumptions. Those
gates remain closed, and no damage or SCC aggregation is authorized.
