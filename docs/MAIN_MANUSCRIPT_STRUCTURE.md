# Main manuscript structure

## Working title

**Expanding the social cost of carbon with AI-assisted, evidence-gated damage modules**

## Central claim

AI can materially expand the feasible scope and reproducibility of integrated-assessment research by auditing literature, acquiring versioned inputs, implementing matched-pulse modules, and stress-testing overlap boundaries across many sectors. The contribution is not that AI makes evidence interchangeable. The scientific value comes from a transparent workflow that distinguishes finished integrations from regional benchmarks, external sensitivities, and results that must remain blocked.

## Abstract

State the omission problem in SCC models; describe the multi-sector evidence-to-code workflow; report the count of outputs by evidence tier; highlight the completed labor integration and narrow precipitation pair test; summarize the regional and external benchmarks; and emphasize that overlap gates currently prohibit a combined headline total. Close with the reproducible architecture and research priorities needed for a joint SCC.

## 1. Introduction

- Explain why omitted damage sectors can bias SCC estimates but careless addition can be worse than omission.
- Frame the project as an economic research design enabled by AI-assisted parallel evidence synthesis and implementation.
- Define the paper's unit of contribution: a validated sector module, bounded benchmark, or explicit negative readiness result.
- State the main scientific finding: implementation feasibility is broad, while additivity is the binding constraint.

## 2. Baseline GIVE and missing-sector inventory

- Describe GIVE's pulse experiment, socioeconomic uncertainty, sector structure, and Ramsey discounting.
- Map existing endpoints and omitted mechanisms.
- Separate physical hazards, economic endpoints, valuation measures, and welfare accounting.

## 3. AI-assisted scientific workflow

Present the repeated pipeline:

1. literature-first candidate search and source-quality screen;
2. version/license/provenance audit;
3. explicit estimand and endpoint boundary;
4. source reproduction before transfer;
5. matched baseline/pulse construction where possible;
6. currency, pulse-year, horizon, and discount harmonization;
7. independent validation and mutation tests;
8. overlap adjudication; and
9. tiered release or fail-closed decision.

Explain human decisions that remain essential: endpoint ownership, acceptable welfare measures, adaptation assumptions, and whether a broad macro response replaces or supplements direct sectors.

## 4. Evidence tiers and aggregation rules

- Define the four ledger categories.
- Explain why “pair-tested” is not synonymous with “additive to every model target.”
- Pre-register that external, regional, and blocked rows never enter a global total.
- Require pairwise compatibility and joint reruns before combining even integrated rows.

## 5. Results

### 5.1 Integrated and pair-tested results

- Heat-related labor productivity: validated same-run expected contribution in the published updated-agriculture GIVE target.
- Annual-maize rainfall quantity: global paired narrow-mechanism result, explicitly not total precipitation-agriculture damages.
- Explain why these two cannot presently be stacked.

### 5.2 External sensitivities

- Fisheries market and combined market/nutrition benchmarks.
- Aggregate water resources.
- Tropical-cyclone persistent-growth benchmark.
- Global forestry/timber market welfare: a reproduced MimiFUND legacy benefit benchmark, with its one-at-a-time range clearly distinguished from a probability interval.
- Interpret magnitudes only in their source-model contracts.

### 5.3 Bounded regional benchmarks

- CONUS heat effects on roads and rail.
- CONUS riverine-flood property damage.
- CONUS freshwater recreation willingness to pay.
- CONUS temperature-related crime.
- Swiss building hail annual-average-loss scenarios under present weather and one +3C pseudo-global-warming experiment, retained in CHF with no SCC conversion.
- Treat model-surface ranges as structural variation, not sampling confidence intervals.

### 5.4 Blocked and negative results

- Air pollution, grid transmission/distribution direct costs, winter-recreation revenue, heat morbidity, education and human capital, freshwater angling, permafrost, Amazon, and wetland-methane feedbacks, global ecosystem services, armed conflict, dengue, and noncoastal migration.
- Show that “blocked” means insufficient separability or transfer evidence, not zero damage.
- For permafrost, distinguish explicit frozen-carbon emissions from FaIR's generic temperature-dependent uptake. Record that a valid contribution needs verified no-feedback baselines, a biogenic-CH4 port, and recursive common-draw baseline/pulse runs. The PerCX 765-versus-775 parameter discrepancy and unused decay argument must be reconciled; the EPA 9–50% combined permafrost-plus-Amazon uplift is not a permafrost estimate and must never be applied as a scalar.
- For Amazon dieback, distinguish explicit forest-stock loss from FaIR's generic land sink and baseline land-use emissions. The META GMST-only hazard omits important interacting drivers, and the released noninteger-duration rule can overshoot its stock even though the audited correction conserves it. Historical conditioning and common-random baseline/pulse recursion remain gates. Neither META's 0.1% result nor a combined EPA uplift transfers as a GIVE scalar.
- For forestry markets, report the negative sign as a legacy market benefit, not a negative physical damage claim. Keep the coefficient documentation/code conflict, old ecological and market calibration, FUND/GIVE scenario mismatch, and fire/pest/storm/carbon overlap visible; the result is not additive.
- For wetland methane, report GIVE's constant natural baseline and the published 30 +/- 13 MtCH4/yr/K MAGICC slope only as interface evidence. A valid feedback must partition or anomaly-anchor the baseline, route biogenic oxidation without fossil CO2, resolve natural-emissions timing, bound the GMST proxy and reversibility, prespecify the negative tail, align MAGICC versions, and use common-draw recursive baseline/pulse runs.
- For Swiss hail, report CHF74 million present and CHF90 million at +3C with the source's rounded +22% change and fixed exposure. Do not linearize per degree, scale geographically, convert currency without a price year, or call the scenario contrast an SCC.
- For adaptation, keep fixed/current-practice agriculture primary. Trend and upper attenuation cases set implementation costs to zero and are gross residual-damage sensitivities, not net SCCs. FrEDI roads, rail, and grid totals already contain specified adaptation costs; inland flooding is a no-additional-adaptation case; CIAM OptimalCost already contains optimized protection, retreat, and residual costs. Retain the OECD agriculture cost range only as a nonallocatable 2005-USD scale.

### 5.5 Mutually exclusive top-down comparator

- Report the Bilal and Känzig global-temperature macroeconomic SCC of 1,206.969 in 2024 international dollars per tonne CO2, with its paper-reported 95% confidence interval of 399–2,015.
- Present it beside—not inside—the bottom-up ledger total.
- State every incompatibility: aggregate GDP/productivity versus sectoral welfare object, international-dollar versus 2020-USD unit, different discounting, different pulse and climate module, and different socioeconomic baseline.
- Prohibit interpreting the level or its difference from GIVE as an omitted-sector residual.

## 6. Overlap and welfare accounting

Use the overlap matrix to discuss agriculture replacement, mortality mediation, nested fisheries estimates, water-resource services, infrastructure boundaries, crime valuation, macro-growth umbrellas, CIAM relocation, and adaptation-cost ownership. Make the no-double-counting rules a primary result.

## 7. Validation and reproducibility

- Hash-bound evidence and immutable upstream releases.
- Independent output validators and adversarial gate-mutation tests.
- Memory-bounded execution and raw-data exclusion.
- Separation of confidential/restricted sources from distributable receipts.
- Exact failure conditions and absence of fabricated values.

## 8. What AI changed

- Increased breadth: many literatures and codebases could be screened consistently.
- Increased iteration speed: source acquisition, schema construction, tests, and documentation were developed together.
- Increased auditability: every promoted claim has a machine-readable gate.
- Did not replace identification, welfare theory, domain judgment, source access, or decisions about acceptable overlap.

Report task history and compute use only from verifiable logs; do not infer unattended working time or productivity from conversation timestamps.

## 9. Limitations and research agenda

- The current ledger is not a completed global SCC.
- Several large source benchmarks rely on broad mortality or macro endpoints.
- Regional FrEDI results cannot be extrapolated globally without evidence.
- Structural uncertainty and adaptation remain incompletely coupled.
- Prioritize a single target model, endpoint registry, global annual paths, and joint uncertainty draws.

## 10. Conclusion

The project demonstrates a scalable way to turn heterogeneous evidence into auditable IAM modules. Its strongest claim is methodological: AI can enlarge the research frontier when every numerical promotion is constrained by provenance, validation, and overlap gates.
