# Next-step priorities

The machine-readable ranking is in `data/derived/next_step_priorities_v1.csv`.

The first priority is not another scalar estimate. It is a single target GIVE configuration with an endpoint registry and pairwise compatibility tests. Without it, even individually sound sector results cannot be assembled into a scientifically coherent total.

Substantive work should then focus on:

1. finishing the multi-crop precipitation replacement with drought, timing, extremes, and irrigation;
2. converting fisheries market welfare to annual matched-pulse paths;
3. reconciling wildfire and nonwildfire PM mortality with baseline GIVE mortality;
4. treating tropical-cyclone macro damages as mutually exclusive until direct channels are decomposed;
5. globalizing bounded regional infrastructure and ecosystem benchmarks only when published response surfaces support it; and
6. converting the winter-recreation revenue benchmark into a welfare measure;
7. completing a nonfatal heat-morbidity direct-medical-cost module; and
8. decomposing grid T&D hazards and separately valuing outage reliability; and
9. completing municipal water, nonfatal dengue, and incremental noncoastal migration interfaces.

The Bilal–Känzig top-down macro result should be maintained as a separate comparison protocol, not “harmonized” through a scalar currency conversion or used to back out missing damages. A valid comparison must preserve and display the five model-object incompatibilities.

Education heat should remain a readiness track until a direct resource or consumer-welfare endpoint and matched school-day marginal climate path are available. Lifetime-earnings conversion is not a shortcut because it duplicates labor and macro channels; air-conditioning adaptation costs must also be separated from existing energy damages.

Freshwater angling should retain the four published CIRA II consumer-surplus NPVs only as original-unit scenario benchmarks. A future marginal module needs annual matched-pulse paths and must remove or jointly model the angling service already present in FrEDI freshwater recreation.

Permafrost remains a feedback-integration problem rather than a damage-sector scalar. Its next defensible milestone is to reconcile the PerCX parameter artifact and unused decay argument, verify emissions baselines, add a biogenic-CH4 port, and validate one-year-lagged baseline/pulse recursion with common draws. The EPA 9–50% result combines permafrost and Amazon feedbacks and is prohibited as a permafrost-only or GIVE-wide uplift.

Amazon dieback likewise remains a recursive feedback-readiness track. The next milestone must resolve generic FaIR land-sink and baseline land-use overlap, update or bound the GMST-only hazard using precipitation, drought, fire, deforestation, and other drivers, apply the tested stock-conserving final installment, and validate historical conditioning and common-random baseline/pulse behavior. META's 0.1% result and combined EPA feedback uplifts are not transferable scalars.

Forestry's reproduced -$0.076953/tCO2 legacy benefit is useful for scale and sign checking only. The one-at-a-time range is not an uncertainty interval. A production module needs current multi-model forest-supply shocks, an open market model, matched GIVE paths, resolution of the MimiFUND coefficient conflict, and explicit separation from fire, pests, storms, forest carbon, and nonmarket services.

Priority reflects expected scientific value times readiness, with overlap risk and required computation treated as costs. It is not a ranking of true damage magnitudes.
