# Cross-sector overlap matrix

The machine-readable matrix is in `data/derived/overlap_matrix_v1.csv`. Its purpose is to decide whether endpoints may coexist in one SCC—not to judge whether each underlying study is credible.

The highest-risk overlaps are:

- **Agriculture:** the narrow precipitation quantity result and labor integration use different agriculture target contracts. The labor receipt expressly prohibits precipitation-agriculture stacking.
- **Mortality:** wildfire smoke, nonwildfire air pollution, fisheries nutrition, dengue mortality, crime loss-of-life, and GIVE temperature mortality cannot be combined without cause-specific attribution and consistent risk functions.
- **Broad macro responses:** tropical-cyclone persistent-growth effects can embed direct agriculture, labor, health, infrastructure, migration, and recovery channels. They are a mutually exclusive sensitivity unless decomposed.
- **Water and ecosystems:** aggregate water resources can include agricultural, municipal, and ecosystem services. Freshwater-quality willingness to pay may include recreational fishing.
- **Migration:** CIAM already includes coastal relocation costs. Only incremental noncoastal real-resource and nonpecuniary costs are missing.
- **Winter recreation:** the numeric result is lost ticket and entry revenue, not welfare. It must be replaced—not added—when consumer surplus, producer profit, substitution, and incidence are estimated.
- **Heat morbidity:** a future module can be conditionally disjoint only if it remains nonfatal, values direct medical resources, and excludes lost wages already represented in labor productivity.
- **Grid infrastructure:** the matched-pulse T&D result values direct repair and replacement but bundles wildfire, coastal flooding, rain, lightning, vegetation, and temperature stressors. It is not outage reliability welfare and cannot be added until infrastructure overlaps are decomposed.
- **Top-down macro comparator:** Bilal and Känzig's aggregate GDP/productivity response can contain every market channel in the bottom-up ledger plus interactions. Its 2024 international-dollar unit, discounting, pulse/climate module, socioeconomic baseline, and damage object all differ from GIVE. It is neither additive nor an omitted-sector residual.
- **Education and human capital:** causal learning effects do not yet provide a matched-pulse welfare endpoint. Lifetime-earnings conversion would overlap labor and macro damages, while school air-conditioning costs can overlap GIVE energy expenditures.
- **Freshwater angling:** CIRA II consumer-surplus NPVs are scenario/era benchmarks in million 2015 USD, not marginal SCCs. Angling is part of the recreation service bundle represented by FrEDI water-quality willingness to pay, so the two cannot be added.
- **Permafrost feedback:** explicit thaw emissions are absent from GIVE's FaIR 1.6.2, but that does not prove every socioeconomic emissions baseline excludes them or establish how PerCX should coexist with FaIR's generic temperature-dependent uptake. Permafrost CH4 also cannot be routed through the fossil-methane port. Permafrost and wildfire carbon feedbacks may ultimately coexist only as distinct emissions sources in the same recursive, common-draw baseline/pulse experiment; separate scalar SCC uplifts cannot be added.
- **Amazon feedback:** an explicit dieback stock is absent from FaIR 1.6.2, but Amazon carbon responses can overlap FaIR's generic temperature-dependent land uptake and baseline land-use emissions. The legacy GMST hazard also omits precipitation, drought, fire, deforestation, and other interacting drivers. Amazon and wildfire feedbacks require distinct stock ownership and one recursive common-draw experiment.
- **Forestry markets:** timber consumer-plus-producer surplus is conceptually distinct from nonmarket ecosystem services and carbon feedbacks, but the underlying forest-stock and productivity changes can already contain fire, pests, storms, or Amazon loss. Addition requires one physical stock ledger and explicit separation of market, nonmarket, and carbon endpoints.
- **Wetland methane:** GIVE already carries a constant natural-CH4 baseline. A feedback must replace its wetland share or add a consistently anchored anomaly, not an absolute flux. Wetland and permafrost CH4 need distinct stocks but the same source-resolved natural-methane and oxidation interface; neither may be routed as fossil methane.
- **Severe hail:** the bounded endpoint is direct Swiss building repair damage only. A future expansion must exclude agricultural hail, vehicles, grids, transport, flood, wind, lightning, and indirect losses or assign each asset and peril once in a joint property registry.
- **Adaptation costs:** free attenuation and duplicate charging are opposite accounting errors. Agriculture trend/upper cases omit implementation costs and therefore are not net. FrEDI roads/rail/grid and CIAM coastal totals already embed specified costs and must be charged once. Inland flooding remains a no-additional-adaptation case. Aggregate OECD agriculture spending cannot be assigned to GIVE scenarios without source-supported mapping.

Resolved or conditionally separable boundaries include wildfire versus nonwildfire emissions, riverine flooding versus CIAM coastal flooding, and heat-only transport infrastructure versus riverine property damage. These physical boundaries still require compatible valuation and joint-run contracts before aggregation.

No pair in the current release is authorized as a new cross-sector headline sum.
